Editing

Editing is the process of selecting and preparing written, visual, audible and film media used to convey information.

The editing process can involve correction, condensation, organization and many other modifications performed with an intention of producing a correct, consistent, accurate and complete work.

The editing process often begins with the author's idea for the work itself, continuing as a collaboration between the author and the editor as the work is created. As such, editing can involve creative skills, human relations and a precise set of methods.

Editorial Positions in Publishing

Editorial Assistants: Reporting to the senior level editorial staff and directors who report to senior executive editors.

Senior executive editors are responsible for developing a product for its final release. The smaller the publication, the more these roles overlap.

The top editor at many publications is known as the chief-editor, executive editor/ the editor.

A frequent and highly regarded contributor to a magazine may acquire the title of Editor-at- large / contributing editor.

Midlevel newspaper editors often manage/ help to manage sections, i.e Business, Sports and Features.

In U.S. Newspapers, the level below the top editor is usually the Managing Editor.

In the Book Publishing Industry, Editors may organise Anthologies and other Compilations, produce definitive editions of a classic author's work (Scholarly Editor) and manage contributions to a multi author book (Symposium Editor and Volume Editor).

Obtaining Manuscripts/Recruiting Authors is the role of a Commissioning Editor in a Publishing House

Sponsoring Editor: finding marketable ideas and presenting them to appropriate authors.

Copy Editors: Correct Spelling & grammar and align writings to house style.

Changes to the publishing industry since the 1980 have resulted in nearly all copy editing of book manuscripts being outsourced to freelance copy editors.

At newspapers and wire services, copy editors write headlines and work on more substantive issues: Accuracy, Fairness and Taste.

In some positions they design pages, select news stories for inclusion. At U.K and Australian Newspapers, the term is that they choose the layout of the publication and communicate with the printer.

These Editors may have the title of layout/ design editor

Editors of Scholarly Books are of three main types:

Acquisitions Editor or Commissioning Editor in Britain: Who contract with the author to produce the copy.

Project Editor/ Production Editor: Who sees the copy through its stages from manuscript to bound book and usually assumes most of the budget and schedule responsibilities.

Copy Editor/ Manuscript Editor: Who prepares the copy for conversion into printed form. For multi-author edited volumes: Before the Manuscript is delivered to the publisher, it has undergone substantive and linguistic editing by the volume's editor, who works independently of the publisher.

Scholarly Journals: Where Spontaneous Submissions are more common than commissioned works, the position of journal editor/ editor-in- chief replaces the acquisitions editor of the book publishing environment.

Roles of Production Editor and Copy Editor remain.

Another Editor is involved in the creation of Scholarly research articles called the Authors' Editor.

Editor works with authors to get a manuscript fit for the purpose before it's submitted to a Scholarly Journal for publication.

The primary difference between Copy Editing, Scholarly Books, Journals and other sorts of copy editing lies in applying the standards of the publisher to the copy.

Most Scholarly Publishers: have a preferred style that usually specifies a particular dictionary and style manual e.g. - The Chicago Manual of Style and the MLA Style Manual or the APA Publication Manual in the USA or the New Hart's Rules in the U.K.

Technical Editing: Technical Writing and Technical Communication.

Technical Editing: involves reviewing text written on a technical topic, identifying usage errors and ensuring adherence to a style guide.

Technical Editing includes: The correction of grammatical mistakes, misspellings, mistyping, incorrect punctuation, inconsistencies in usage, poorly structured sentences, wrong scientific terms, wrong units and dimensions, inconsistency in significant figures, technical ambivalence, technical disambiguation, statements conflicting with general scientific knowledge, correction of synopsis, content, index, headings and subheadings, correcting data and Chart Presentation in a research paper/ report and correcting errors in citations.

Large Companies dedicate experienced writers to the technical editing function.

It helps if the technical editor is familiar with the subject being edited.

The "technical" knowledge that an editor gains overtime while working on a particular product/ technology does give the editor an edge over another who has just started editing

content related to that product/ technology.

Essential general skills/ attention to details, the ability to sustain focus while working through lengthy pieces of text on complex topics, tact in dealing with writers and excellent communication skills.

Paid Editing: may be provided by specialized editing firms/ by self employed (freelance editors).

Editing Firms may employ a team of in-house editors or rely on a network of individual contractors. Both firms are able to handle editing in a wide range of topics and genres, depending on the skills of individual editors. The services provided by these editors may be varied.

The proofreading, copy editing, online Editing, developmental editing, editing for search engine Optimization (SEO).

Self-employed Editors: Work directly for clients (e.g. authors/ publishers) offer their services through editing firms/ both.

They may specialise in a type of editing (e.g. copy editing) in a particular subject area. Those who work directly for authors and develop professional relationships with them are called Authors' Editors.

FARIN RUWA WATERFALLS

The Farin Ruwa falls is a waterfall found in the central region of Nigeria. Farin Ruwa is a Hausa language word meaning "white water." Farin Ruwa is a description of the nature of the falls by the local inhabitants of the Farin Ruwa area. It is among the highest waterfalls in Nigeria. It is significant among Africa's falls when the total height covered by the waterfall is considered.

The Source of the Farin Ruwa falls is found in Nasarawa state, from whence it gushes down over the edge of the Nasarawa. During its descent from the Jos plateau, the water drops a total height of about 150 metres (492 ft). This drop is higher compared to the more popular Victoria falls, which drops a height of around 108 metres (354 ft) during its descent.

The height covered during the descent of the falls is very great, so that the descending water crashes during its way down the Jos plateau escarpment. The falls gradually turn white in colour as a result of the height covered during descent. The falls as a result of its white colour appears in the distance to the inhabitants of the Farin Ruwa area like white smoke on the mountains. This white nature of the falls earned it the name Farin Ruwa from the inhabitants.

The mountainous nature of the region surrounding the Farin Ruwa falls induces heavy rainfall from clouds passing over the region during the rainy season from March - November. As a result, there is an increase in the volume of water of the falls crashing from the mountains during the rainy season. The mountains around the falls are at the edge of the Jos plateau. There is a drop in the volume of water of the falls during the dry season from December - March, but it is for a short while as the falls recovers its water volume again during the rainy season.

Heart Transplant

The world's first heart transplant was performed by Dr. Chris Barnard in Cape Town on 3rd Dec,1967. The patient Louis Washkansky was suffering heart failure at the time and Dr Barnard took the chance to operate him. After that, Barnard became an international celebrity and went on to perform more than 10 other heart transplants with one of the recipient surviving 23 years

CAT Scan

The Computed Axial Tomography Scan/ CAT was developed by Cape Town physicist Allan Cormack and Godfrey Hounsfield.

He provided the mathematical technique for the CAT Scan, in which an x-ray source and electronic detectors are rotated about the body and the resulting data is analysed by a computer to produce a sharp map of the tissues within a cross-section of the body. This resulted in a Nobel prize in the “Physiology or Medicine” category.

Q20

Invented in 1950 in Pinetown, Kwazulu-Natal by a Mr. Robertson, as a product to displace water from distributor caps on the old VW Beetle, which was notorious for stalling in wet weather. It was an effective water repellent which kept rust at bay, eased squeaky door hinges and made it easy to release rusted/ seized nuts and bolts.

The secret behind Q20 is that it is heavier than water.

Q20 has a specific gravity of 1.154 and can therefore displace water whose specific gravity is 1. When Q20 is sprayed on water, it sinks to the bottom, acting as a water displacer and lubricant for the problem area.

In the under-privileged Delmas 75 neighborhood of Haiti's Capital, Port-au-Prince, residents are keeping their hands clean and preventing the spread of the novel Coronavirus. Thanks to a brand new contraption, courtesy of teen inventor and recycler Wens Dimanche.

The 18-yr old, who received acclaim in Haiti in 2019 for his ingenious remote-control leaders and trucks made of scrap metal, saw a need to help his neighbors wash their hands without contaminating the faucet with the Coronavirus. The teenager, who has no formal training in electronics, took a white plastic bucket, filled it with water, attached a mechanical faucet to dispense the water and linked it to a blue plastic pedal, which powers it on/off. When people need to wash their hands, they can simply step on the pedal to release water - it is an electric pedal, switched on by attaching the cord. When you lift your foot off the pedal, the water stops flowing."

To power his invention, the teen used a cell phone battery which is rechargeable, and to keep things safe and easy, the Pedal can be detached from the bucket when it needs to be recharged. The invention is currently located on a metal table in his backyard where neighbors are free to stop by to wash their hands at any time.
